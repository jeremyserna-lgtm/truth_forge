# Friendship and Sex: A Philosophy

**Date**: December 20, 2025
**Context**: A conversation about Adam, processed in real time

---

## The Core Principle

Sex doesn't exist as something so profoundly special that it overrides all other truths. It's not a trump card. It's not sacred in a way that gets to control other people's freedom. It's just a thing people do.

And I refuse to let my feelings become a cage around someone else's choices.

---

## The Adam Situation

Adam had sex with my ex-boyfriend. But Adam didn't know who my ex was, because I never told him.

When it happened, Adam called me immediately. He felt bad. He didn't want to have done that. During the sex, my name came up, and my ex flipped out. Adam told him: "I have no idea who you are. I'm Jeremy's best friend."

It created something twisted that Adam couldn't understand. And he just felt bad.

I was relieved but torn apart. I cried my eyes out.

But this was the only way all the truths could coexist.

---

## Why I Never Told Adam Who My Ex Was

Adam wished I had told him beforehand so he could avoid my ex. I told him I could never do that and be happy.

Because:

1. **I don't own that relationship.** My ex is his own person. Adam is his own person. I don't control what they do.

2. **Adam should be free to have sex with whoever he wants.** My ex or not. Whether he knows or not. That's Adam's right.

3. **I won't transfer my baggage.** If I tell Adam who my ex is, I'm making Adam responsible for avoiding my pain. That's not his weight to carry.

4. **My ex is attractive.** I wanted to have sex with him. I did. Of course Adam would want to when he saw him. They're guys who do that.

---

## What I Did Instead

I held two things at once:

- **It's going to hurt if it happens.** True.
- **He has the right to do whatever he wants.** Also true.

Neither cancels the other. I don't collapse one into the other. I don't pretend it doesn't hurt. I don't pretend he shouldn't have done it.

Both are true. I hold both.

---

## The Result

Adam slept with my ex. We moved on from it as friends with barely any second thoughts. Because it all came down to the truth of what it was and the way that it was. And that it's passed.

I feel validated about:

- Being a good friend—not burdening Adam with information that would become his baggage
- Being someone an ex-boyfriend would react against—meaning the relationship meant something
- Not visiting something on my best friend that would make him carry weight that isn't his

---

## The Current Impasse (December 2025)

Adam and I aren't speaking. Since Thanksgiving.

After his party, I sent a text: "Wow, this is a party that people are not going to get naked and have sex."

Adam said that was rude. I didn't respond. He said, "I guess you just don't care." I didn't respond.

Because I don't know how to respond. All I said was true. It wasn't a party where that happens. I was just noticing it—noticing that I was there, with him, at a party different from what I'm used to.

He turned it into being mad at me.

---

## The Impasse Analyzed

**What happened:**
- I observed something true
- Adam heard a judgment
- I couldn't apologize because I didn't mean anything wrong
- A false apology wouldn't be honest
- He can't make me believe I was wrong when I wasn't

**The loop:**
Adam wants me to admit I was wrong. I can't, because I wasn't. The loop says we're stuck forever.

But I'm not in that loop. I'm in a different one: **I want to talk to my friend.**

---

## How I'll Break the Loop

I'm going to push past all his bullshit and focus on him as the thing I want.

Not the argument. Not the apology I can't give. Just: I miss my friend and I want to talk to him.

He's going to watch me do it. He's going to see me care about him more than I care about being right. More than I care about the fight.

That's the lesson. Not a lecture. Just me, doing the thing.

He can meet me there or he can stay stuck. That's his choice. But he's going to see me make mine.

---

## What This Proves

If we could move through the ex-boyfriend thing—something that actually hurt, something that actually mattered—we can move through a comment about a party.

We already know how to do this. We already did the harder version.

---

## The Right I'm Claiming

I have the right to be done with my past relationship however I want.

Some people might think this is crazy. They might say Adam isn't a good friend. They might say I should be upset or estranged.

But they don't get to decide what I care about. I do.

And I decided: **the friendship is what matters.** The relationship I'm in now—with Adam—is the one I'm going to protect. Not the one I left.

I'm not performing jealousy because someone thinks I should. I'm not holding a grudge because the script says that's what happens.

I'm just done with the past. And present with the friend.

---

## The Philosophy, Summarized

- Sex doesn't override other truths
- People have autonomy over their bodies and choices
- I don't control others
- I can hold my feelings without making others responsible for avoiding them
- My feelings are mine; other people's rights are theirs
- The living friendship matters more than the dead relationship
- I push past the noise to get to the person

That's not crazy. That's clarity.
