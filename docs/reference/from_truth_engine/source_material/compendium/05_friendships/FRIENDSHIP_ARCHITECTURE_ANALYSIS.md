## Pattern 0: The Container

**The Foundation:** Jeremy's home functions as a safe, bounded, honest space where friends can come.

**What It Looks Like:**
- Taylor uses his bathroom as a retreat between dangerous worlds
- Butch arrives in 2 minutes when invited
- The orgy happens in his space
- Friends shower, hang out, share vices, have sex - all within bounded safety

**The Container Philosophy:**

> "I don't want my living room to be a prison. I can create a level where a murderer can come in here, but I want to have a living room where I can sit and actually have an honest conversation with that person."

**What the Container Holds:**
- Sex, vice, drugs, vulnerability, truth, purity, honesty - all in one
- The opposite of the dangerous outside world
- A space where people can be vulnerable because the boundaries are clear

**Access Requirements:**
- Pass the fidelity filter
- Understand complexity
- Those who need simple narratives get filtered out

**Meaning:** Friendship happens within architecture. The container isn't just where friends gather - it's what makes the depth of connection possible.

## Pattern 4: Protecting Friend Autonomy

**The Pattern:** Not burdening friends with information that would become their baggage.

**The Adam Test Case:**

Jeremy never told Adam who his ex-boyfriend was.

Adam later had sex with that ex (unknowingly).

**Why Jeremy Didn't Tell:**

1. **Adam's freedom matters more** - He should be free to have sex with whoever he wants
2. **Baggage isn't transferable** - Making Adam responsible for avoiding Jeremy's pain isn't fair
3. **He doesn't own the relationship** - His ex is his own person, Adam is his own person
4. **They're guys who do that** - Of course Adam would want to when he saw him

**The Result:**

They moved past it with "barely any second thoughts."

**The Principle:**

> "I refuse to let my feelings become a cage around someone else's choices."

## Pattern 5: Holding Multiple Truths

**The Pattern:** Neither truth cancels the other. Both exist simultaneously.

**The Adam-Ex Situation:**

- **Truth 1:** It's going to hurt if it happens.
- **Truth 2:** He has the right to do whatever he wants.

Jeremy held both. Didn't collapse one into the other. Didn't pretend it didn't hurt. Didn't pretend Adam shouldn't have done it.

**The General Principle:**

Friendship doesn't require resolving contradictions. It requires holding them.

**Examples:**
- Taylor is a drug dealer who navigates dangerous worlds AND maintains core honesty
- Butch's messages declined sharply AND the relationship deepened
- The ex-situation hurt AND Adam did nothing wrong

## Pattern 6: The Living Relationship Over the Dead One

**The Pattern:** The present friendship matters more than the past relationship.

**The Decision:**

> "The friendship is what matters. The relationship I'm in now—with Adam—is the one I'm going to protect. Not the one I left."

**What This Means:**

- Not performing jealousy because the script says to
- Not holding grudges the conventional way
- Being done with the past while being present with the friend

**The Claim:**

> "I have the right to be done with my past relationship however I want."

Others don't get to decide what he cares about. He does.

## Pattern 9: The Fidelity Filter

**The Pattern:** People who understand complexity can enter. Those who need simple narratives get filtered out.

**Who Passes:**
- Those who can handle sex, vice, drugs, vulnerability, truth, purity, honesty coexisting
- Those who don't need to simplify or judge
- Those who can hold contradictions

**Who Gets Filtered:**
- Those who project onto his behavior
- Those who need him to match their framework
- Those who can't handle the container's contents

**Evidence - The Orgy:**
- Curated for people who understand
- "The guys that I am" - attractive, reasonable, good at sex, don't normally go to orgies
- Those who can be in sexual space without imposing

## Pattern 10: Factory Default Honesty

**The Pattern:** Close your eyes, do what heart says, outcome will be honest and caring.

**Origin - Conversations with Taylor:**

> "I can give Taylor understanding in ways that aren't injections of anything new, but are repeating things he already found and did in ways that tell him what the truth is, and that he is honest."

**What This Means:**

- Reflecting truth back rather than imposing new truths
- Recognizing that friends already know their own answers
- The role is to surface, not to instruct

**The Deviation Warning:**

When you deviate from factory default, it distorts and hurts.

## Pattern 12: Pushing Past to Get to the Person

**The Pattern:** When impasses form, push past the conflict to get to the friend.

**The Current Adam Impasse (December 2025):**

Not speaking since Thanksgiving. Jeremy sent a text about Adam's party ("this is a party that people are not going to get naked and have sex"). Adam said it was rude. Jeremy didn't respond.

**The Analysis:**

| What Happened | What Each Wants |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Jeremy observed something true | Adam wants him to admit he was wrong |
| Adam heard a judgment | Jeremy can't, because he wasn't |
| No false apology possible | A false apology wouldn't be honest |

**The Loop Others Would Be In:**

"He wants me to admit I was wrong. I can't. We're stuck forever."

**The Loop Jeremy Is In:**

"I want to talk to my friend."

**The Resolution Strategy:**

> "I'm going to push past all his bullshit and focus on him as the thing I want. Not the argument. Not the apology I can't give. Just: I miss my friend and I want to talk to him."

**What Adam Will See:**

Jeremy caring about him more than being right. More than the fight. That's the lesson - not a lecture, just doing the thing.
