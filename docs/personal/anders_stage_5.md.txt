A Working Map of Anders (for people who actually want one)
This isn’t a résumé, a dating bio, or a highlight reel.
It’s context — the kind most people skip, and then wonder why they don’t quite understand me.

The questions I wish people would ask (and the answers)
How do you actually think?
I think in systems, patterns, and second-order effects.
I’m less interested in what something is than why it works, who it serves, and what breaks if you scale it.
Small talk doesn’t bore me — unexamined assumptions do.

What do you care about more than you let on?
Integrity of thought. Emotional literacy. Consent — not just sexually, but intellectually and relationally.
I care about people being honest about what game they’re playing, even if it’s messy.

What kind of intelligence do you respect?
The kind that listens before it speaks.
The kind that can change its mind without collapsing its identity.
The kind that doesn’t confuse dominance with clarity.

Where do people usually misread you?
They mistake my calm for detachment.
My precision for criticism.
My openness for availability.
I’m not distant — I’m selective about where I place depth.

What’s your relationship to power?
I don’t chase it. I understand it.
I’m more interested in how power flows than in holding it for its own sake.
That’s why I tend to see dynamics other people miss — and why shallow hierarchies irritate me.

What are you actually building?
Spaces — internal and external — where people can think, feel, and explore without pretending.
Some of those spaces look like businesses.
Some look like conversations.
Some look like intimacy.
The throughline is coherence.

What do you need from people close to you?
Curiosity instead of assumption.
Directness without theatrics.
The willingness to ask real questions and sit with real answers.
I don’t need agreement.
I need engagement.

What exhausts you?
Being flattened.
Being “figured out” too quickly.
Having to translate nuance for people who aren’t listening anyway.

What lights you up?
When someone tracks the thread.
When a conversation deepens instead of widens.
When mutual respect replaces performance.

How do you know when you’re safe with someone?
When I don’t have to manage their projections.
When silence isn’t awkward.
When complexity isn’t treated like a threat.

The simplest truth
I’m not difficult — I’m layered.
And I meet people exactly as far as they’re willing to go.
If you’re reading this and nodding instead of skimming, that already tells me something.
