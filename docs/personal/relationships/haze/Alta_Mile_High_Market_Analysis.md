## PART I: META-ANALYSIS
### Understanding Apartment Competition Dynamics

### What Makes a Competitor? A Systems Approach

Traditional real estate analysis defines competition through **geographic proximity**—apartments within a 1-2 mile radius competing for the same renters. This linear model misses the complex, networked reality of how modern urban renters make decisions.

Competition in multifamily housing operates across **five distinct but overlapping layers**:

#### **Layer 1: Geographic Proximity** (The Traditional Model)
- **Mechanism:** Physical distance and neighborhood identity
- **Range:** 0.5-2 mile radius
- **Logic:** Renters compare properties they can easily visit in one tour day
- **Assumption:** People choose neighborhoods first, then properties
- **Limitation:** Ignores infrastructure that collapses distance

#### **Layer 2: Transit Network Connectivity** (The Network Model)
- **Mechanism:** Shared transit infrastructure creates non-contiguous competitive sets
- **Range:** All properties within 5-10 minute walk of the same transit line
- **Logic:** Light Rail/bus lines create "transit neighborhoods" that compete across physical distance
- **Example:** A property near Federal Blvd station competes with one near Broadway station if both serve downtown commuters on the same line
- **Key insight:** **Connectivity trumps proximity**—two properties 5 miles apart but on the same Light Rail line compete more directly than two properties 1 mile apart with different transit access

#### **Layer 3: Employment & Activity Nodes** (The Destination Model)
- **Mechanism:** Major employers, stadiums, universities create reverse-commute competitive sets
- **Range:** 15-30 minute commute radius from major nodes
- **Logic:** Renters optimize for commute time to work/entertainment, not residential location
- **Major Denver Nodes:**
  - Downtown/CBD (office workers)
  - Sports Complex: Empower Field (Broncos), Ball Arena (Nuggets/Avalanche), Coors Field (Rockies)
  - University of Denver, MSU Denver, CU Denver
  - Denver Tech Center
- **Key insight:** Properties compete based on **commute efficiency to shared destinations**, not just location

#### **Layer 4: Infrastructure Corridors** (The Flow Model)
- **Mechanism:** Highways and major roads create linear competitive markets
- **Range:** Properties along I-25, I-70, US-6 corridors
- **Logic:** Car-dependent renters prioritize highway access over neighborhood character
- **Key insight:** **Mobility infrastructure defines competitive boundaries**—a property with easy I-25 access competes with other I-25 corridor properties regardless of neighborhood

#### **Layer 5: Lifestyle & Demographic Segmentation** (The Identity Model)
- **Mechanism:** Renters self-select into lifestyle communities that match their identity
- **Range:** City-wide or metro-wide
- **Logic:** "People like me live in places like this"
- **Segments:**
  - Urban creatives (arts districts, music venues, galleries)
  - Young professionals (walkable, nightlife, modern)
  - Families (schools, parks, safety)
  - Remote workers (space, home office, quiet)
  - Car-free advocates (transit, walkability, bike infrastructure)
- **Key insight:** **Identity-based competition crosses all geographic boundaries**—a renter seeking "arts community" will compare properties across the entire metro area

---

### The Synthesis: Multi-Dimensional Competitive Space

**Competition is not a circle on a map—it's a multi-dimensional space where properties occupy positions along multiple axes simultaneously.**

A property's true competitive set emerges from the **intersection** of these five layers:

```
COMPETITIVE SET = Geographic Proximity 
                  ∩ Transit Network 
                  ∩ Employment Access 
                  ∩ Infrastructure Corridors 
                  ∩ Lifestyle Segmentation
```

**Example for Alta Mile High:**
- **Geographic:** Sun Valley, West Denver, Baker fringe
- **Transit:** All properties on W/D Light Rail lines (Federal, Decatur, Sheridan stations)
- **Employment:** Downtown commuters, Sports Complex workers
- **Infrastructure:** I-25 South corridor, Morrison Rd corridor
- **Lifestyle:** Arts/culture seekers, music enthusiasts, price-conscious luxury, car-free capable

This creates a competitive set that includes:
- Nearby apartments (traditional competition)
- Properties 3-5 miles away on the same Light Rail line (transit competition)
- Properties near other downtown-serving transit nodes (employment competition)
- Properties along I-25 corridor (infrastructure competition)
- Arts-focused communities anywhere in Denver metro (lifestyle competition)
